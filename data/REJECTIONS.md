# Independent second pass over the 42 Phase 2e rejections

This document is an independent second pass over the forty-two Lane B candidates that Phase 2e rejected. The merge table in `data/ADJUDICATION.md` gave every rejection the same sentence — that synonym, compression, ordering, or unsupported absence did not meet the inclusion rule — so a reader cannot tell why any one candidate fell or check the call. The original adjudicator's reasons are unrecoverable, and this pass does not try to reconstruct them.

The verdicts and reasons below are this pass's own. Each candidate was judged again against the edition's inclusion rule: record a variant when, at the same point of the discourse, the witnesses differ in a way that changes the doctrinal claim, the historical detail, the rhetorical framing, or the evidence of what was heard. T remains derived, never a fifth independent witness. Where a locus is already an apparatus entry, that is stated as the reason. `Uphold` means the rejection still looks right; `disagree` means the candidate should have been included, under the type named. A handful of disagreements is the point of the audit, not a failure of it.

## Disagreements for the editor

Ten candidates meet the inclusion rule on this pass. They are listed here with enough of the collation to act on without returning to the table.

**S07.3 (omission).** Bullock and Clayton independently record a prediction that acquaintance with God will end persecution of Joseph ("all persecution. agst. me will cease"; "you will cease to persecute me"). Woodruff and Richards are both taking notes in S07 — false prophets, rights of conscience — and neither has this causal close. It is not a shortening of that material, and it is not V051, which is a later wish for an archangel's trumpet so that telling the story would make persecution cease forever. The entry would record a two-against-two omission of a claim about why Joseph is persecuted.

**S13.2 (omission).** Three eyewitnesses apply "fear and trembling" to the Father's own kingdom-making. Woodruff has the rest of the sentence ("see the father do what, work out a kingdom") and drops the phrase. That is an omission from a witness present at the point, not a shorter report of a long paragraph, and neither V009 (before/when worlds) nor V010 (kingdom upon kingdom) records it. The phrase is a biblical image laid on the Father's work; its presence or absence changes the rhetorical — and arguably the doctrinal — picture of how God obtained a kingdom.

**S14.2 (theological).** Woodruff states that understanding "the whole" will take "a long time after the grave." Bullock, Richards, and Clayton deny that "it is all to be comprehended in this world," which is a claim about this life, not a schedule for the next. Bullock's neighbouring "learn Salvation beyond the grave" can be read as the object of learning rather than as a postmortem classroom; it does not say "a long time after." The modal and locative force therefore differs, and T follows the this-world denial rather than Woodruff's duration.

**S17.2 (historical).** In the polyglot-Bible comparison only Bullock gives a number: the German "corespends the nearest to the revelations that I have given the last 14 years." Clayton has the correspondence without the span; Woodruff and Richards have the Jacob/James demonstration without the revelatory-career claim. Fourteen years is a dated historical detail, and T prints it, so the received text makes the German an external check on the whole career from about 1830. The later S34 German-and-fourteen-years sentence is a second use of the same span, not a substitute for recording it here.

**S22.4 (theological).** At the close of the laws-for-intelligences argument the reports disagree on who gave the teaching. Bullock: "Inspiration of the H S." Woodruff: "the revelations of Jesus Christ." Clayton: "words of eternal life that are given to me," giver unnamed. Richards has already dropped out of the sentence. That is the same kind of source-difference V036 was retained for, and it is not covered by V019 (the end the laws serve) or V075 (the honey). T prints both Clayton's ungiven words and Bullock's Holy Spirit, which is a reception consequence worth having in the eyewitness entry as well.

**S24.1 (theological).** Who must obey, and what counts as obedience, is not the same across the reports. Woodruff specifies baptism; Clayton limits the damned-unless-they-obey class to those who "have not obeyed the gospel in the flesh"; Bullock and Richards require the gospel of "all the Spirits" / "All spirits" without that mortal filter. T follows Clayton, so the received text quietly restricts the scope of postmortem obedience. None of V079, V080, or V020 records this. It is the strongest miss in the rejection list.

**S26.6 (theological).** Woodruff's S26 definition of the unpardonable sin is a prerequisite: it cannot be committed "until He receives the Holy Ghost." Bullock, Richards, and Clayton all draw a mortal-life boundary: not "after the dissolution of the body," but "in this world." Those are different necessary conditions. V025 already quotes Woodruff's fuller S28 sequence (Holy Ghost, heavens opened, know God, then sin); it does not record that at the S26 statement Woodruff substitutes that prerequisite for the this-world boundary the other three give, and T follows the boundary.

**S32.1 (historical).** Woodruff includes "Children" among Joseph's dead and uses plural "Fathers"; Bullock has "a Far. Bror. Friends"; T follows Bullock, only pluralizing brothers. Richards and Clayton have no list. A name in the family of the dead is a historical detail the inclusion rule names as such, not a stylistic expansion of "friends."

**S34.1 (reception).** Bullock's "the Baptism of Water witht. the Baptism of Fire & the H G. attending it are necy" is syntactically ambiguous as to what is necessary. Woodruff says John's water baptism is "good for nothing" without Christ's. Richards says water, fire, and Holy Ghost "are inseparably connected." T prints "is of no use: they are necessary," which is both Woodruff's worthlessness of water alone and a resolved necessity claim no one eyewitness states as a pair. That is T shaping the received baptism doctrine, and it belongs under the reception rule already used for V107–V121.

**S34.2 (theological).** Richards alone grounds Christ's coming baptismal authority in "having the keys." Bullock and T have "who has the power"; Woodruff has Christ coming to "administer" with no basis named. In this discourse keys are an office, not a synonym of power, and the difference is the sort of modal/scope change the theological type is for. Clayton has already broken off.

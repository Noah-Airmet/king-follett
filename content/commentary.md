## A. Character and reliability of the witnesses

The four eyewitness reports differ not only in content but in kind. **Thomas Bullock (B)** produced the fullest continuous report and the only eyewitness record that runs through every editorial section. It preserves such material as the ladder image (V011), the sun-denial image (V025), and the address to Alexander Campbell (V031), but its compressed syntax can leave force uncertain: V003, for example, can look declarative until W, R, and C make the intended refutation clear. Its “age to end” at V018 may be a slip, but the edition retains it because, if deliberate, it gives intelligence a terminus inconsistent with the rest of the sentence (Smith, pp. 36, 170, 280).

**Wilford Woodruff (W)** produced a refined journal account rather than an assigned conference report. It preserves memorable formulations, including God “that holds the worlds” (V002), the devil's first-person “I am a savior and can save all” (V024), and a vision of children reigning on thrones without added stature (V030). It also gives the fullest eyewitness statement about sealing children and the dead (V020), material absent from T because Woodruff's report was not used in the 1844 composite (Smith, pp. 38, 58–60, 79, 294 n. 71).

**Willard Richards (R)** is brief and telegraphic, yet it preserves singular details: the falling tub of rock at V001, “Gnolom” at V032, “Lot fell on Jesus” at V088, and “all the intelligence of a god” at V029. At V006 Richards writes both “everlasting burning” and “everlasting power,” where B has power and W has burnings. That combination is some evidence that both terms may have been spoken, but the suggestion that Richards “caught both words” remains a hypothesis, not a demonstrated reconstruction (Smith, pp. 138, 290 n. 38).

**William Clayton (C)** supplies a substantial independent parallel to Bullock until his record breaks off. Among its distinctive formulations are deification “in order to save yourself” (V007), the Father “on a planet as Jesus was in the flesh” (V004), and element existing “from the time he had” (V015). Because T was made from B and C, a Clayton reading that appears in T has passed through an editor as well as a witness; it has not acquired another independent attestation (Smith, pp. 58–61, 73–76).

## B. The nature of God: form and divine history

The reports cluster several consequential differences around the nature and history of God. At **V003**, B's “he was God from the begin of all Eternity & if I do not refute it” is syntactically ambiguous. W's “We suppose,” C's “We have imagined,” and R's “refute the Idea” establish that the conventional belief is being introduced for rejection. T chooses C's unambiguous “We have imagined,” so the received text resolves a difficulty in B rather than reproducing it (Smith, pp. 51, 275, 289 n. 31).

At **V004**, B says the Father “dwelt on a Earth same as J C himself did”; W says the Father was once “on an earth like us”; and C says he “was once as one of us and was on a planet as Jesus was in the flesh.” R omits the passage. All three extant reports describe prior mortality, but C makes corporeality especially explicit. T follows B's “earth” and Christ parallel, while later Utah editors replaced the passage with the familiar statement that God “was once as we are now” (Smith, pp. 31, 268 n. 79, 289 n. 33, 290 n. 26).

At **V002**, W's God “holds the worlds” has a plural scope; B and C refer to “this world” and its orbit or sphere. T follows B and thus gives the received text the narrower grammatical object. The difference may imply dominion over multiple creations in W, although a plural noun in one report cannot by itself settle the intended cosmology (Smith, pp. 62, 249, 290 nn. 27–28).

## C. Theosis: human deification

The reports agree that the audience must learn a divine pattern, but characterize its process differently. At **V005**, B has “grace to grace,” W and R have increasing “capacity,” and C has movement “from exaltation to exaltation.” These metaphors emphasize gift, developed ability, and conferred status respectively. T combines all three in a series that no eyewitness records, making this locus a compact demonstration of its amalgamating method (Smith, pp. 31, 138, 275).

At **V006**, B describes “everlasting power,” W “everlasting burnings,” and C enthroned glory; R combines burning and power. The reports could preserve different portions of a longer utterance, but they could also reflect reportorial paraphrase. T, compiled without W or R, combines C's enthroned glory with B's everlasting power and leaves out the “burnings” language later restored in the Utah text (Smith, pp. 138, 290 n. 38).

The purpose and endpoint also vary. At **V007**, C alone adds “in order to save yourself,” while T pluralizes B's singular formulation into “Gods yourselves.” At **V019**, B speaks of “one glory upon another,” W of being “exhalted with” God, C of a privilege to “advance like” God, and R of enlargement. T joins C's advancement and B's graduated glories. These differences allow several models of progress, but the reports do not authorize choosing one isolated phrase as the complete doctrine (Smith, pp. 31, 37, 290 n. 37, 294 n. 67).

## D. The premortal council and divine succession

At **V010**, the four reports emphasize different consequences of the Son's work: B has “Kingdom rolling upon Kingdom,” W describes the Father taking a higher exaltation and the Son taking his place, C says the Father's glory is exalted, and R says God is “gratified” in the exaltation of his creations—though JSP flags *gratified* as possibly *glorified*. T fuses B and C and omits W's explicit succession. Read together, the reports suggest a dense account of divine advancement; any harmonized cosmological scheme remains an editorial inference (Smith, pp. 32, 290 n. 42; JSP R n. 2).

The council material contains two different variant loci. At **V024**, W alone casts the devil's claim in the first person—“I am a savior and can save all”—while T combines C's “would not be saved” with B's “all” (Smith, pp. 39, 66, 296 n. 75). At **V088**, R alone writes “Lot fell on Jesus,” while B says the council “gave in for” Jesus and T follows B. If *lot* is the common noun, Richards may preserve the language of a selection by lot; Smith connects it to council elections, but the precise mechanism remains an interpretation rather than a fact established by the note (Smith, p. 66, 296 n. 75).

## E. Creation, element, and the eternity of matter

All four reports reject creation from nothing, but **V015** shows how their formulations differ. B alone says glory dwells in element and says elemental principles “exist eternally.” W emphasizes indestructibility and reorganization; R explicitly writes “no beginning no end.” C alone says element “had an existence from the time he had” and then describes organization and reorganization. Read strictly, C's pronoun could put God and element on the same temporal footing, but it does not itself state that God had a beginning (Smith, pp. 34, 248, 292 nn. 59–60, 293 n. 62).

T prints B's indwelling-glory clause and C's “from the time he had” in sequence. As V118 records, it then follows C's relative-duration formulation while omitting B's and R's explicit no-beginning language. The received paragraph is therefore an editorial fusion whose implications are not identical to any one eyewitness report.

## F. “Immortal” and “coequal”

At **V017**, B reads “the mind of man is as immortal as God himself”; W, R, and C each read “coequal with God himself,” with C specifying “the intelligent part.” *Immortal* directly asserts continuing duration; *coequal* can suggest parity or standing. The three-to-one distribution makes *coequal* the better-attested word at this locus, and Smith treats it as the archetypal reading, but the reports cannot demonstrate whether only one term was spoken (Smith, pp. 36, 139–40, 279).

T follows B with “immortal” here, then prints that the spirits “existed co-equal with God” a few lines later. Both words consequently entered the received text rather than one simply replacing the other. Later editors altered the formulation again, including a substitution of “co-eternal,” illustrating how editorial attempts to clarify the metaphysics became part of the textual history (Smith, pp. 139–40, 171, 318).

## G. Salvation, the unpardonable sin, and punishment

At **V022**, the witnesses use four terms for inward suffering: B's “condemner,” W's “damns,” R's “disappointment,” and C's “torment.” R's term is the mildest, while B's is the most forensic; their common ground is that the mind participates in its own condemnation. T combines C and B into “his own tormenter, and … his own condemner” (Smith, p. 39, 282). W's nearby denial that hell fire exists is a separate unique reading, V085, and should not be folded into V022 as though all were variants of one sentence.

At **V033**, B sets two outcomes beside each other: some rise to God's “everlasting burning,” and others to the damnation of their own filthiness, “same as the lake of fire & brimstone.” R independently preserves only the positive proposition, “God dwells in everlasting burnings”; W and C have no counterpart. T retains B's pairing but turns the lake into a comparison of intensity—“as exquisite as”—rather than identity. The juxtaposition in B can support a reading of divine burning and punitive filthiness as contrasting experiences, but that theological synthesis rests chiefly on one report (Smith, pp. 42, 286).

## H. Methodological observations

No single report is superior at every point. B gives the broadest coverage; W often supplies extended thematic material; R can preserve singular details in very compressed form; and C offers an important parallel until it breaks off. Their disagreements are the reason to preserve the reports rather than collapse them.

Four-way divergence may mark a concept that was difficult to record, but that is a hypothesis, not a rule. V006, V010, V019, V022, and V024 show how differently longhand reporters could render a common locus. Some differences may reflect successive phrases in the delivery; others may be compression, memory, or each writer's vocabulary. The texts rarely permit those causes to be separated.

Likewise, a unique reading is neither automatically suspect nor automatically authentic. Its presence establishes what one document says; its absence elsewhere limits corroboration. The same restraint applies to apparent combinations such as R's “burning & everlasting power” at V006: the combination is evidence worth weighing, not a license to reconstruct an otherwise unattested sentence.

Finally, T must be read as an editorial event. At some loci it selects one report (V002, V003); at others it fuses B and C (V005, V013, V015); elsewhere it supplies a consequential transition or alteration no eyewitness has (for example V111 or V122). It is indispensable for understanding the discourse's reception precisely because it is not independent evidence for the occasion.

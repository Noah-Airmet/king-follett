The King Follett Discourse survives without an authorial manuscript or a stenographic transcript. This edition therefore presents five texts side by side: four eyewitness records of the sermon delivered at Nauvoo on 7 April 1844 and the first published composite made from two of them. Its purpose is not to manufacture a seamless speech, but to let readers see both the substantial agreement of the reports and the differences that affect what the discourse can be taken to say.

The sigla are **B**, Thomas Bullock's conference minutes; **W**, Wilford Woodruff's journal; **R**, Willard Richards's entry in Joseph Smith's journal; **C**, William Clayton's conference minutes; and **T**, the version printed in the *Times and Seasons* on 15 August 1844. B, W, R, and C are eyewitnesses. T is **derived**, not a fifth independent report: Thomas Bullock and William Clayton made it from their own notes, and its agreement with either source is therefore not corroboration. It is included because it shows how the received text began to take shape.

## Base text and sections

Bullock supplies the lemma, or base text, because his is the fullest eyewitness record and extends through all {{stats.sections}} thematic sections. That choice is practical, not a claim that Bullock always preserves the best wording. His report is one fallible longhand record among four; at many loci another witness is clearer, fuller, or substantively different. The parallel texts and apparatus are therefore integral to reading the edition, not supplements to an otherwise definitive Bullock text.

The sections are editorial units made by aligning the complete reading text of each report by topic. Together they partition every text without gaps or overlaps. Where a witness moves, merges, or omits material, the alignment records that fact rather than forcing its words into Bullock's order. The section headings summarize content; they do not claim that Joseph Smith supplied an outline.

## What the apparatus records

The apparatus contains {{stats.total_variants}} entries. Its governing rule is quoted from the apparatus metadata:

> Record a variant when, at the same point of the discourse, the witnesses differ in a way that changes the doctrinal claim, the historical detail, the rhetorical framing, or the evidence of what was heard.

The types are likewise defined in the data:

- **Theological:** “The variant changes the doctrinal claim, its scope, or its modal force (e.g. 'immortal' vs 'coequal'; 'world' vs 'worlds'; 'God' vs 'Gods'; 'suppose' vs a declarative).”
- **Historical:** “The variant changes a factual detail, a name, a number, a scripture cited, or an event.”
- **Rhetorical:** “An image, metaphor or framing device is present in some witnesses and absent or different in others (the ladder, the ring, the sun).”
- **Unique:** “Substantive content stands in exactly one eyewitness; the others are recorded as om.”
- **Omission:** “A passage present in two or more eyewitnesses is lacking in another witness that is present at that point, and the omission is more than a scribe's compression. The entry must say why.”
- **Reception:** “T only. Record separately wherever T departs from all eyewitnesses in a meaning-bearing way, or sides with one eyewitness against the others at a point that shaped the received text. T's reading remains in eyewitness entries as well.”
- **Scribal:** “A cancellation or an insertion in one report bears on the sense. The cancelled reading is quoted in that witness's reading as 'kept text (canc. cancelled text)'; where a scribe struck more than one span at the point, they are separated by '; '.”

The exclusions are also explicit:

> - spelling, abbreviation, punctuation and capitalisation, except where JSP itself flags them or where they carry sense, in which case the entry says why
> - synonym-level substitution or word order with no change of sense
> - a witness merely being shorter, unless the omission criterion is met

The current distribution is {{stats.types.theological}} theological, {{stats.types.reception}} reception, {{stats.types.unique}} unique, {{stats.types.rhetorical}} rhetorical, {{stats.types.historical}} historical, {{stats.types.scribal}} scribal, and {{stats.types.omission}} omission entries. Each significance note is deliberately hedged: it identifies what a difference could mean without treating an imperfect record as proof of the words spoken.

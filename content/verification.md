title: What this edition claims
slug: verification
updated: 2026-09-15
---

This edition accepts the Joseph Smith Papers transcriptions as authoritative. It does not claim an independent examination of the manuscripts. Where JSP flags its own transcription with a “TEXT: Possibly …” footnote, the edition preserves that uncertainty in `data/footnotes.json` as a textual note and links it to the relevant apparatus entry when the reading bears on one.

The January 2026 site reported that an earlier AI-assisted pass had checked selected readings against JPG scans. Those images are no longer held in the repository, and the verdicts cannot be reproduced. The notes survive on the affected entries as `legacy_verification` for transparency, but this edition does not rely on them as evidence and does not repeat their claims of confirmation, plausibility, or legibility.

Several contested readings especially affect interpretation:

- **V017, “immortal” / “coequal.”** B reads “the mind of man is as immortal as God himself”; W reads that man existed “in spirit & mind coequal with God himself”; R reads “Mind of man co-equal with God himself”; and C specifies “the intelligent part” as “coequal with God himself.” T follows B with “immortal” at this locus, then preserves “co-equal with God” a few lines later. The reports therefore support both words in the received tradition, but three eyewitnesses support *coequal* at the disputed locus and one supports *immortal*. Smith treats *coequal* as the likely archetypal reading and traces later editorial substitutions, including “co-eternal” (Smith, pp. 36, 139–40, 171, 279, 318).

- **V015, Clayton's “from the time he had.”** B says that glory dwells in element and that elements “exist eternally”; W says elemental principles cannot be dissolved but may be reorganized; R says “no beginning no end”; C alone writes that “element had an existence from the time he had,” followed by the claim that elemental principles can be reorganized but not destroyed. T places B's indwelling-glory clause beside C's relative-duration wording, while a related reception entry, V118, notes that it omits B's and R's explicit no-beginning formulations. Read strictly, C could place God and element on the same temporal footing, but the pronoun's implication should not be converted into a claim that the report explicitly says God began. Smith quotes and discusses the Clayton passage in relation to the eternity of element (Smith, pp. 34, 248, 292 nn. 59–60, 293 n. 62).

- **V088, Richards's “Lot fell on Jesus.”** R alone writes “Lot fell on Jesus”; B has “the grand council gave in for J. C.,” while W and C have no counterpart. T follows B: “the grand council gave in for Jesus Christ.” JSP capitalizes the transcribed word, but capitalization does not decide whether Richards meant the common noun *lot*. In context, Smith connects the phrase with a council decision or election (Smith, p. 66, 296 n. 75). The evidence supports the language of casting lots as an interpretation; it does not establish the exact procedure of the reported council.

- **V029, Richards's “all the intelligence of a god.”** R alone says that the child possesses “all the intelligence of a god.” B instead says the child's “debt is paid” and that no damnation awaits; W has no counterpart at this locus, and C's section is a clerk's summary rather than reported words. T follows B and omits Richards's phrase. Smith discusses the sermon’s teaching about resurrected children but does not specifically adjudicate this wording (Smith, pp. 41, 156, 285, 297 n. 80). It therefore remains a unique Richards reading, neither corroborated nor disproved by the other reports.

Each entry identifies a point at which the reports differ in a meaning-bearing way. Its compact form follows this pattern:

> *lemma* ] B reading ; W reading ; R reading ; C reading ‖ T reading

The **lemma**, before the closing square bracket, is normally quoted verbatim from Bullock, the base text. If Bullock has no counterpart, the entry identifies the eyewitness from which the lemma comes. After the bracket, semicolons separate the readings of B, W, R, and C. **om.** means that a witness has no counterpart at that point; it does not mean that an editor has deleted words.

The double divider **‖** separates the four eyewitness reports from T, the derived *Times and Seasons* text. A T reading shows what the first published composite did with the eyewitness material. It is evidence for reception and editing, not an additional witness to the sermon. In this edition, **derived** means that a text was made from earlier reports: T descends from B and C, so its agreement with them is genealogical, not independent corroboration.

Each entry also has a type. **Theological** variants alter a doctrinal claim, scope, or force; **historical** variants alter facts, names, numbers, citations, or events; **rhetorical** variants alter an image or framing device; **unique** entries preserve substantive content in one eyewitness only; **omission** entries record a consequential absence in an otherwise present witness; **scribal** entries expose a meaning-bearing insertion or cancellation; and **reception** entries isolate a consequential editorial choice made by T.

In a scribal reading, **canc.** reports words visibly cancelled by the scribe. For example, `had power (canc. hath)` means that the manuscript retains evidence of an earlier *hath*, but the reading text follows the uncancelled *had*. Multiple cancelled spans are separated within the parenthesis by semicolons.

## Worked example

V005 (S11), a theological entry, reads:

> *from grace to grace until the resurrection.* ] from grace to grace until the resurrection. B ; from a small capacity to a great capacity to the resurrection of the dead, W ; by going from a small to great capacity. R ; from a small degree to another from exaltation to exaltation— C ‖ from a small degree to another, from grace to grace, from exaltation to exaltation, T

Bullock's words furnish the lemma. Woodruff and Richards describe movement in “capacity,” while Clayton describes degrees and “exaltation.” T then combines Bullock's “grace to grace” with Clayton's two formulations. The divider makes the editorial act visible: no eyewitness writes T's three-part series, so it should not be cited as four reports agreeing on that wording. William V. Smith discusses this fusion in his account of the received text (Smith, pp. 31, 138, 275).
